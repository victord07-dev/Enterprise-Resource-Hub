Enhance the ERP workflow so that Sales Orders, Inventory, and Supply Chain modules are properly integrated while preventing negative inventory and maintaining clear demand tracking.

When a Sales Order is confirmed, the system must automatically perform an inventory availability check for each product SKU in the order.

If sufficient stock exists in the warehouse, the system should create a Stock Reservation record for that quantity. Reserved stock should reduce the Available Stock but must not reduce Total Stock until goods are actually dispatched.

If the ordered quantity exceeds available stock, the system should:

Reserve the available quantity from inventory.

Calculate the shortage quantity.

Automatically generate a Purchase Request in the Supply Chain module for the shortage amount.

Link the Purchase Request to the originating Sales Order.

The Sales Order itself should not contain any drop-shipment or fulfillment type selection, because the sales team should only record customer demand and not decide procurement or logistics methods.

In the Supply Chain module, when the procurement team converts a Purchase Request into a Purchase Order, they must select a field called Fulfillment Type with the following options:

Warehouse Replenishment

Direct Supplier Delivery (Drop Shipment)

If Warehouse Replenishment is selected, the workflow should be:

Purchase Order → Supplier Delivery → Goods Receipt Note (GRN) → Inventory Stock Movement (+) → Inventory Updated.

If Direct Supplier Delivery (Drop Shipment) is selected, the workflow should be:

Purchase Order → Supplier Dispatch → Delivery Challan → Customer Delivery.

In the drop shipment scenario, warehouse inventory must not increase or decrease, because goods move directly from the supplier to the customer.

The Inventory Products List interface must display clear operational stock information with the following columns:

SKU / Item Code

Product Name

Warehouse

Total Stock (physical stock currently in warehouse)

Reserved Stock (stock reserved by confirmed Sales Orders)

Available Stock (Total Stock − Reserved Stock)

Incoming Stock (quantity expected from open Purchase Orders marked as Warehouse Replenishment)

The system must never allow negative inventory.

If stock is insufficient, the shortage must be handled through Purchase Requests and Purchase Orders, not by reducing inventory below zero.

Users should be able to click on the Reserved Stock number to view a breakdown of which Sales Orders reserved the stock. The detail panel should show:

Sales Order Number

Reserved Quantity

Expected Delivery Date

Reservation Status

When a Delivery Challan is generated and dispatch is confirmed, the system must:

Create a Stock Movement record (type: Sales Dispatch).

Reduce Total Stock.

Reduce or close the corresponding reservation.

When a Goods Receipt Note (GRN) is confirmed for warehouse replenishment, the system must:

Create a Stock Movement record (type: Purchase Receipt).

Increase Total Stock.

Update the product’s moving average cost.

Add visual indicators in the inventory product list to highlight:

Products where Available Stock is below the Reorder Level.

Products where Available Stock is zero.

This workflow ensures that Sales expresses demand, Inventory tracks stock availability and reservations, and the Supply Chain team controls procurement decisions such as warehouse replenishment or direct supplier delivery.
